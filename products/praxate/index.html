Everything we watch for, and what we don't.

Left runs on customer premises today. Right is being built, and nobody here will promise it on a call. If a competitor lists all of these with no such split, ask which are running on a customer's premises this week.

Live today

  • People counting — headcount per area and premises.
  • Areas — enter, exit, time spent, any shape you draw.
  • Restricted areas — entry alerts on the hours you choose.
  • After-hours activity — anything outside your working day.

007 / Praxate Bay

Every bay counted. Every driver placed.

Commercial parking runs on guesswork: a counter at the ramp, a guard waving an arm, and a customer circling B2 for six minutes. Bay reads every space on every level and sends the next car to one of them by name.

Three things happen. None involve the driver.

We map every bay once. Cameras read the bays, not the cars.

Our team walks the site and lays out the rows; each bay becomes its own small area on a camera, drawn once and signed off against a rendered contact sheet. A parked car does not move, so a bay camera is sampled every few seconds rather than tracked frame by frame — that is what makes a hundred cameras affordable on one server. A screen at the entrance names a bay and points at it. Your guards stop directing traffic.

  • We count the empty bays — not capacity minus vehicles. One missed car never becomes a space that doesn't exist.
  • If we cannot see, we say so — a bay whose camera is down reads unknown and is never offered to a driver.
  • A promised bay is held for the two minutes a driver takes to reach it, so the next car is sent somewhere else.
  • Video stays on your site — one server in your rack, 100+ cameras, continuous recording stays on your NVR.
